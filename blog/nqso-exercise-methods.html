How to exercise NQSOs: five methods, compared.

Deciding when to exercise non-qualified stock options gets most of the attention. How you exercise matters too. The method determines how much cash you need, how many shares you keep, and how much of your net worth stays tied to one company.

Alex J. Potenza, CFP®, CPWA®
Vice President & Director of Financial Planning

First, the tax that doesn't change

Whichever method you use, the tax at exercise is the same. The spread, meaning the market value of the shares at exercise minus the exercise price, is ordinary income. For employees it is reported on your W-2 and is subject to Social Security and Medicare tax as well as income tax. Your cost basis in any shares you keep becomes their market value on the exercise date, and later gains or losses are capital.

Employers withhold on that income, often at a flat supplemental rate that may be lower than your actual bracket. The gap can show up as a balance due in April. Our post on RSU tax withholding explains the same problem for RSUs.

What the method changes is everything else: the cash you put in, the shares you hold afterward, and your exposure from that point on.

The five methods

1. Cash exercise

You pay the exercise price and the tax withholding from your own cash and keep every share. This maximizes the shares you own, which also maximizes your concentration in one stock. It makes sense when you have cash available and a deliberate reason to hold more of the company.

2. Cashless exercise (same-day sale)

Your broker exercises the options and immediately sells all of the shares. The proceeds cover the exercise price and withholding, and you receive the rest in cash. No cash is needed up front and your exposure to the stock does not increase. Because the sale happens at about the exercise value, there is usually little additional gain or loss beyond the ordinary income.

3. Sell-to-cover

The broker sells only enough shares to pay the exercise price and withholding. You keep the remaining shares. This is a middle path: no cash out of pocket, but you add to your position. Remember that withholding may not cover the full tax, so the amount sold may be too small to cover what you will actually owe.

4. Stock swap

If your plan allows it, you pay the exercise price by delivering shares of company stock you already own, sometimes by attestation rather than physical transfer. This lets you exercise without cash and without selling shares on the market. The tax treatment of the surrendered and new shares, including how basis and holding periods carry over, is technical. Have your CPA review it before you use this method.

5. Net exercise

Some plans let the company withhold shares that would otherwise be issued, to cover the exercise price and taxes. You receive the net shares. It works like sell-to-cover without a market sale, but it is available only if your plan provides for it.

How to choose

Four questions usually decide it:

  • How concentrated are you already? If company stock is already a large share of your net worth, cashless exercise avoids adding to it.
  • Do you have the cash, and is this the right use of it? A cash exercise can tie up savings that other goals need.
  • Can you trade? Blackout windows, insider trading policies, and, for affiliates, Rule 144 may limit when and how shares can be sold. Insiders often schedule exercises and sales under a 10b5-1 plan.
  • What is the tax picture this year? The ordinary income lands in the year of exercise regardless of method. Spreading exercises across years can matter more than the method.

Hypothetical example: Meet "Marcus," a vice president at a public company whose stock is already a large share of his net worth. He has one NQSO grant nearing expiration. Holding more shares would deepen his concentration, so a cashless exercise lets him capture the value and diversify in one step. A colleague with the same grant but little company stock might reasonably choose sell-to-cover instead.

Don't let options expire unexercised

NQSOs typically expire ten years after grant, and sooner after you leave the company. Options that are in the money on their expiration date are lost if no one exercises them. Some plans and brokers offer an automatic exercise feature at expiration and some do not. Know which applies to you, and put every expiration date on a calendar.

Keep the records your CPA will need

After a sale, the 1099-B from your broker often shows a cost basis that leaves out the income already taxed on your W-2. If that number is used as-is, the same income can be taxed twice. Keep the supplemental statement from your stock plan administrator. Our post on what to give your CPA covers the fix.

Common mistakes

  • Choosing sell-to-cover by default and adding to an already concentrated position.
  • Assuming the withholding on exercise covers the full tax bill.
  • Missing an expiration date.
  • Using a stock swap without understanding its basis and holding-period rules.
  • Reporting the 1099-B basis without adjusting it for income already on the W-2.

Frequently asked

Frequently asked

What is the difference between cashless exercise and sell-to-cover?+

A cashless exercise sells all of the shares at exercise and pays you the net cash. Sell-to-cover sells only enough shares to pay the exercise price and withholding, and you keep the rest. Both avoid paying cash up front; only sell-to-cover adds to your stock position.

How are non-qualified stock options taxed when exercised?+

The spread between the market value at exercise and the exercise price is ordinary income in the year of exercise. For employees it appears on the W-2 and is subject to payroll taxes. Shares you keep take a cost basis equal to their value on the exercise date.

Does the exercise method change the tax on NQSOs?+

The ordinary income at exercise is the same whichever method you use. The method changes how much cash you need, how many shares you keep, and your later exposure to gains or losses on the shares you hold.

What is a stock swap exercise?+

A stock swap uses shares of company stock you already own to pay the exercise price, if your plan allows it. It avoids using cash or selling shares on the market. The basis and holding-period rules are technical, so review it with your CPA first.

What happens if my NQSOs expire?+

Options not exercised by their expiration date are generally forfeited, even if they are in the money. Some plans offer automatic exercise at expiration and some do not. Check your plan and track each grant's expiration date, which is usually earlier if you leave the company.
