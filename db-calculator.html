What this model does not do

  • Assumes no employees. Eligible employees generally must be covered, which changes the cost of the plan.
  • Uses a simplified actuarial method. The assumptions are calibrated against sample 2026 illustrations from a third-party plan administrator and generally land within about 10% of them. An enrolled actuary sets the real contribution each year, usually as a range, using IRS mortality tables and your plan's design.
  • Assumes the plan starts this year and that you work continuously until the plan retirement age.
  • Caps the contribution at your compensation. For self-employed owners, the contribution also reduces the earnings it is based on, which this model does not iterate.
  • Defined benefit plans are a commitment. Contributions are generally required each year, and administration and actuarial fees apply.
